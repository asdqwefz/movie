How to host a watch party without a browser extension

Browser extensions are the most common way to sync a movie, but not everyone wants to install one. Here are four ways to watch together without one, and what each costs you.

Last updated: November 22, 2026

Quick answer: Use a built-in watch party from your streaming service, a browser-based site, screen sharing, or a dedicated app such as MoviesParty, where everyone installs one app and joins with a room code.

Why people avoid extensions

An extension needs permission to read and change the pages you visit, it can break when a streaming site updates, and it has to be installed in a supported browser on every friend’s computer. If you manage a school, work or shared computer, installing one may not be allowed at all.

Where MoviesParty fits

MoviesParty is the last option in that table. It is a Windows app (macOS is on the way), so nothing is installed into your browser and no extension needs to read your pages. You create a room, send the code, and everyone watches together in the app, with voice, video and text chat beside the player.

How to choose

  • Everyone has the same streaming service: use its built-in watch party if it has one.
  • Your group mostly watches YouTube or web video: a browser-based site is the quickest.
  • Only one person has the movie: screen sharing, with the quality limits described in our watch party vs screen sharing guide.
  • Everyone is in different places and you want one room with a code: a dedicated app.

Frequently asked questions

Can I watch Netflix together without an extension?

Netflix does not have a built-in group viewing feature, so most tools use an extension or screen sharing for it. A dedicated app avoids both by syncing each person’s own playback.

Is a watch party without an extension free?

It can be. MoviesParty’s free plan includes 1080p, public rooms and chat, and other tools have their own free tiers.
